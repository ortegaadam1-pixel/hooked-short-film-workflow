# Story tests — evidence before confidence

Statuses: REVISE, PROVISIONAL, SUPPORTED, NOT APPLICABLE with reason. A paper rationale supports a development decision; only observed footage/playback supports execution claims. Audience comprehension requires actual viewer evidence. Do not manufacture a PASS to keep production moving.

## Premise tests

1. **Obvious answer:** List at least two plausible alternatives where applicable. Why not use the door, ask for help, wait, leave, or use an established power? If an easy solution remains and no other uncertainty matters, revise the premise. Account for alternatives naturally; do not stack arbitrary barriers.
2. **Why care:** Name a specific desire, relationship, responsibility, embarrassment, loss, or moral cost. Identify the behavior or situation establishing it. A cute design, generic sadness, or narration declaring high stakes is insufficient.
3. **Connected hook:** Put opening question and final answer side by side. Does the hook expose or cause the main problem? Catching a gift immediately cannot sustain “will it fall?” for the remainder. If the question changes, show the causal connection.
4. **Choice causes outcome:** Remove the protagonist's decisive action. Would the same ending happen? If yes, investigate passivity/coincidence. Deliberate helplessness in tragedy or satire can be appropriate if meaningful.
5. **Earned solution:** Identify where each essential rule, clue, skill, object, or relationship is established. Remove unintroduced rescue powers. A twist is optional; earned resolution is essential.
6. **Distinctive experience:** What particular dilemma, relationship, image, insight, or comic mechanism makes this more than “a character has a problem and succeeds”? This is editorial judgment, not a novelty score.
7. **Share/replay hypothesis:** Who might value sending it to whom, and why? Does a detail gain meaning on rewatch? Do not force a loop, withhold resolution, or add engagement bait to satisfy this question.

## Beat and edit tests

- **Information:** Can the relevant evidence be perceived at actual speed and size? Locate the image/audio that communicates it.
- **Uncertainty:** At each turn, what remains unresolved? Does narration, staging, or an early reveal accidentally answer it?
- **Consequence:** Does each attempt change choices or stakes? Remove interchangeable attempts and unrelated complications.
- **Deadline:** Is it established and consequential? If removing the clock changes nothing, it is decoration. A clock is not required.
- **Hold:** What develops during the pause? Changing emotion or anticipated sound can justify it; inert remainder cannot.
- **Cut:** Why here? Check information, rhythm, gaze, action phase, screen direction, prop state, and audio.
- **Sound:** Is requested narration present? Does picture complement rather than merely duplicate it? Speech intelligibility requires listening.
- **Ending:** Is the promised outcome answered, caused, and emotionally readable? Does the film continue past its strongest final moment without purpose?

## Visual-development tests

Apply [VISUAL_DEVELOPMENT.md](VISUAL_DEVELOPMENT.md) alongside script tests:
- Three opening concepts must differ in staging, reveal, viewpoint, scale, or relationship, not merely adjectives.
- Identify a specific memorable story image/action; a style label or saturated palette alone is insufficient.
- Trace visual progression through the script's turns. Repetition without new action, feeling, or information needs redesign.
- Locate picture-led evidence for essential events. Narration adds meaning instead of substituting for absent action.
- Identify the final visual payoff and its earlier setup; a literal callback is optional.
- Each panel must answer why it is worth looking at and why viewers want what follows; the last panel must justify satisfaction.
- Review actual phone-size images and full motion/sound sequences. Readability, visual attraction, story utility, and feasibility are separate findings.

A quiet, restrained panel can satisfy these tests. Spectacle, fast cutting, identical shot-size alternation, and constant movement are not mandatory. Results remain provisional when only self-reviewed.

## Performance and finish tests

Apply [PERFORMANCE_AND_FINISH.md](PERFORMANCE_AND_FINISH.md): actual opening delivery comparison; natural full spoken performance; expression/key-pose and color/light references; timed phrase/reveal/reaction relationship; every-cut playback; dedicated stall/rush review; material animation-defect repair; finished representative two-shot method test before bulk footage; complete exact-export picture/sound review. Use N/A with a reason for narrator-specific checks in intentionally unnarrated films.

A smooth join cannot pass from matching stills alone. An attractive face cannot pass acting without motion. A checked form cannot pass unheard narration. Missing method playback blocks bulk polished generation, not document development. These are craft acceptance rules, not evidence of audience preference or studio equivalence.

## Required evidence record

| Claim | Planned evidence | Actual evidence/location | Reviewer and method | Status | Specific repair |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Viewer understands obstacle | Close view and failed attempt | Not yet observed | Paper self-review | PROVISIONAL | Test complete rough sequence |

Forbidden substitutes: “the prompt says it”; “the image is cinematic”; “export succeeded”; “I know what it means”; “there is a hook shot”; “runtime fits.” None alone proves audience experience.

## Decision

REVISE before generation if the chosen story has an unexplained easy solution, disconnected hook, missing required narration, incoherent causality, unearned payoff, or cannot fit intelligibly. If the rough sequence fails to communicate obstacle/payoff, return to script/staging. Unobservable execution stays PENDING/PROVISIONAL. User review is the default before polished generation; explicit autonomous authorization permits a provisional run, not false validation claims.
