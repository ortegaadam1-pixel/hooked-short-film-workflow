# Research basis and evidence limits — v2 review draft

Compiled 2026-10-24. The workflow combines professional craft instruction with original production recommendations. It has not been experimentally validated as a retention system. No source establishes a universal cut interval, perfect hook duration, guaranteed emotional response, or viral formula.

## Primary and official craft sources

- [Pixar in a Box — Storytelling](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling): character, structure, visual language, and film grammar curriculum. [Visual-language lesson](https://www.khanacademy.org/v/visual-language) discusses how framing, scale, shape, color, and value communicate importance and feeling. Applied here to readable composition; not proof that a particular frame retains viewers.
- [Disney Animation — Story](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/story/): storyboarding addresses narrative alongside emotion, expression, timing, staging, and framing. Applied here to develop the sequence before expensive polish. Page accessed 2026-10-24; no publication date displayed.
- [Disney Animation — Layout](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/layout/): rough staging and cinematic decisions consider viewpoint, emotion, shot relationships, clarity, and timing. Applied here to motivated camera choices and adjacent-shot planning. Page accessed 2026-10-24; no publication date displayed.
- [Disney Animation — Animation](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/animation/): timing, anticipation, follow-through, staging, and weight contribute to performance. Applied here to distinguish empty delay from purposeful preparation or response. Page accessed 2026-10-24; no publication date displayed.
- [Pixar — Incredible Cinematography](https://prod.renderman.pixar.com/stories/incredible-cinematography), January 24, 2019: filmmakers describe coordinated lighting and camera language, and contrast between dynamic action and stable domestic scenes. Applied here to visual progression, intensity contrast, and lighting that expresses intent. Historical case study; software-version details are not current app instructions.
- [Adobe — Introduction to Video Editing](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/edit-a-video.html): presents Walter Murch's priorities of emotion, story, rhythm, eye trace, and spatial relationships. Applied here to editing by dramatic purpose. Its separate sizzle-reel advice is not generalized into a rule for narrative films. Page accessed 2026-10-24; no clear publication date displayed.
- [Adobe — Continuity Editing](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/ideas/what-is-continuity-editing-in-film.html): explains eyelines, screen geography, matching action, eye trace, and audio bridges. Applied here to maintaining understandable sequences. Page accessed 2026-10-24; no clear publication date displayed.

## Empirical research

[Bruckert, Christie, and Le Meur, Where to Look at the Movies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.13378), submitted February 26, 2021, presents an eye-tracking dataset and relationships between editing and gaze patterns. Directing gaze is not equivalent to proving enjoyment, empathy, completion rate, or commercial success. The workflow does not derive numerical retention targets from this paper.

[Original Mini Drama Producer playbook](https://github.com/ai-playbooks/realistic-mini-dramas/blob/main/PLAYBOOK.md), v1.1.1 as read in this conversation, supplied the starting map of VideoExpress controls. ## Original recommendations

Premise comparisons, obvious-solution challenges, viewer information maps, opening variants, fresh-viewer questions, evidence statuses, and focused audience-learning iterations are this workflow's practical synthesis. They are heuristics, not experimentally established thresholds. v2 removes the fixed 45–55-second default as a planning target: use the shortest effective story with margin under 60. Actual audience performance requires finished-film evidence and real responses.

## Visual-development extension

VISUAL_DEVELOPMENT.md operationalizes the preceding Pixar/Disney composition, staging, performance and sequence-design guidance through opening comparisons, a memorable story image, visual progression, picture-led events, panel annotations and a visual payoff. The requirement for three visual concepts and the two panel questions are this workflow’s editorial heuristics, not source-proven formulas or guarantees of attention. Actual effectiveness needs rough playback, completed-film review, and viewer evidence.

## What is and is not validated

The previous Santa production completed technically but the user rejected its suspense, clarity, pacing, and missing narration. v2 treats those as failures to prevent, not proof that the prior workflow worked. This draft has documentary checks and explicit paper stress tests in REVIEW.md. No v2 animatic, fresh-viewer study, finished-film trial, or release-performance result has been completed. No claim that this is the best possible or guaranteed-viral workflow is supported.
