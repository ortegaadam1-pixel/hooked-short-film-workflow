# Performance and finishing — v2.3

Required companion to [SYSTEM_PROMPT.md](SYSTEM_PROMPT.md), [VISUAL_DEVELOPMENT.md](VISUAL_DEVELOPMENT.md), and [PRODUCTION_TEMPLATE.md](PRODUCTION_TEMPLATE.md).

Disney-quality craft is the user's benchmark for this animated film. It is an ambition, not a certification, rendering preset, prompt adjective, or demonstrated capability of this workflow. Apply the underlying storytelling, performance, continuity, and finish standards to every genre and medium; do not force Disney's visual treatment on other styles. These requirements are editorial recommendations informed by the sources in RESEARCH.md, not validated retention thresholds.

Apply [SMOOTH_EDITING.md](SMOOTH_EDITING.md) as the mandatory execution detail for Sections 1, 3–7. Use its independent narration cleanup, native-speed assembly, connected action/reaction, sequence-level rhythm review, and caption checks. Reuse an approved voice/performance rather than repeating an audition without a demonstrated need.

## 1. Direct the spoken performance

For a narrated film, compare two short deliveries of the actual opening with its intended pictures. Change intention, emphasis, or phrasing rather than only voice identity. Use the same narrator where practical; select a voice only within the user's authorized identity constraints. Record the selection and concrete listening findings. For an intentionally unnarrated film, mark narrator-specific fields N/A and apply appropriate performance checks to dialogue or visual acting.

Write a performance brief: storyteller viewpoint, relationship to the audience, emotional progression, emphasis, pronunciation, pace, purposeful silences, and words that must remain exact. Direct playable intentions rather than requesting generic excitement. A restrained genre may need subtle changes rather than a loud delivery.

Judge whether the opening line and image jointly establish an interesting situation and reason to continue. Avoid generic preambles, false urgency, robotic cadence, forced drama, and accidentally revealing the solution. Record and time the complete selected narration performance before committing to final footage. Temporary audio supports rough development; changed final delivery requires renewed picture timing. Do not rush speech to meet the runtime ceiling.

Listen to the actual track for natural phrasing, breath/pauses, pronunciation, intelligibility, consistent identity, and emotional credibility. Fix defects before building the final edit around them. Keep narration, dialogue, music, ambience, and effects independently adjustable when the available tools permit.

## 2. Establish consistent art and believable acting

Create a compact reference package: stable character proportions/costume; useful face, expression, and key-pose references; important props and geography; and representative color/light keyframes for the opening, development, climax, and payoff. Reuse existing suitable references rather than regenerate unnecessarily. Match the selected medium, including intentional stop-motion cadence or graphic simplification.

Plan each important action as intention → readable preparation where needed → decisive action → consequence/reaction. Specify eyeline and expression changes, contact, weight, and follow-through. Do not demand broad movement from a quiet emotional beat.

Inspect actual moving clips for identity drift, unintended morphing/flicker, sliding feet, floating objects, broken hand contacts, incorrect prop ownership, weak reactions, and unmotivated camera movement. Review lip sync when applicable. Reject or repair defects that distract from the story or obscure meaning; intentional stylization is not automatically a defect. A beautiful still cannot approve a performance.

Compare shots together for palette, light direction, exposure, material/line treatment, scale, and character design. Correct unintended changes while preserving motivated shifts in mood or location.

## 3. Time picture and speech together

For each important spoken phrase record the cue, what the viewer must already know, what appears during it, and what changes afterward. Pictures can anticipate, reveal, contradict, or reinterpret words deliberately; do not illustrate every word mechanically.

Demonstrate essential physical events at readable size and speed. In the Santa case, show the obstruction's effect and Santa's response before the intended problem-solving interval. Do not use narration to claim an obstacle that the pictures fail to communicate. Preserve time for the audience to understand the problem before revealing the solution.

Check the actual synchronized sequence, not merely the table. Record timecoded findings on early/late reveals, obscured details, competing action during key lines, and missing reactions.

## 4. Design and inspect every join

For every adjacent pair record:
- Actual outgoing/incoming source ranges and cut position in the edit.
- Last outgoing and first incoming usable states: pose, action phase, gaze, prop state/ownership, screen direction, and intended focal position.
- Camera movement/velocity, framing/geography, light/color continuity, and any intentional discontinuity.
- Dialogue/narration continuity, ambience continuity, and any prelap or sound bridge, with actual timing when used.
- Why the cut or transition occurs and what the viewer learns or feels.

Choose a clean cut, match on action, reaction cut, sound bridge, dissolve, or other transition for its dramatic function. A dissolve is not a default repair for mismatched staging. Match enough action to preserve continuity without replaying an action unintentionally.

Watch each join in context at normal speed with sound. Frame inspection helps diagnose a defect but cannot establish that a join feels smooth. Repair distracting jumps, audio clicks/gaps, accidental repeated movement, broken geography, and unwanted exposure/color changes. Log the observed result, not just the planned match.

## 5. Run a dedicated pacing pass

A scene is a dramatic unit and may contain several shots. Generated source duration never determines retained shot duration. Do not impose a universal cut interval, one sentence per shot, or constant motion.

A useful change may develop within a continuous shot; finding a new idea does not require adding a cut. Audio silence alone never authorizes a linked picture deletion. Start with native-speed complete action; retiming to fill a speech window requires the specific exception record in SMOOTH_EDITING.md. If a passage feels rushed, compare a breathing-room version; choose the tighter version only when it actually improves clarity and feeling.

For every retained interval, identify the change in action, information, emotion, or anticipation. Locate the exact point at which the intended information becomes readable. Cut or redesign any remainder that stops developing meaning. Preserve a reaction or silence while it continues to reveal thought or build anticipation.

Record timecoded stalls, rushed comprehension, redundant inserts, repeated action, and overlong farewells. When an interval stalls, compare a tighter version at normal speed and keep the version that best preserves clarity and emotion. If no stalls are found, record how the complete sequence was reviewed; do not invent an alternative-cut test.

## 6. Prove the method with a finished two-shot sample

After the selected development package and within production authorization, finish two adjacent representative shots before bulk polished footage. Prefer a difficult story-bearing action/reaction or continuity join, not two easy beauty shots. Use the intended identity, medium, actual selected voice where speech is required, and planned music/ambience/effects.

The sample must demonstrate: readable story action; credible performance; stable design; controlled timing; a convincing join; natural and intelligible speech where applicable; and coherent picture/sound. Record the exact artifact/version, observed strengths, material defects, and repairs.

This is a practical method test, not audience validation. If material defects remain, repair the method or redesign coverage before bulk generation. If browser tools cannot produce or inspect the sample, mark it PENDING and report the concrete limitation. Autonomous production authorization removes routine permission pauses; it does not waive this evidence requirement or justify generating the whole film with an unproven method. Existing footage can supply the sample when suitable.

The two-shot sample tests a specific join, not the whole rhythm. Also review the connected setup/action/result/reaction sequence under SMOOTH_EDITING.md before final rendering. Reuse existing footage or the authorized rough sequence; this is not permission for extra media spending.

## 7. Finish and judge the exact film

Build appropriate synchronized physical effects and continuous ambience, and use music and silence for the selected emotional arc. Avoid canned intensity that masks weak storytelling. Listen for speech masking, abrupt changes, clipping/distortion, inconsistent voices, missing contacts, and distracting transitions. Do not claim measured audio compliance without measurement.

Watch the exact export continuously at normal speed with sound, plus phone-size and muted checks for their separate purposes. Record timecoded findings for opening, sustained interest, acting, joins, picture/word timing, pacing, mix, visual consistency, and earned conclusion. Resolve material defects and rewatch the changed export before creative approval.

A technically completed export with creative review incomplete is WORKING/REVIEW. Missing playback remains PENDING; sampled frames or a written checklist cannot replace it. Record who actually watched/listened and how; do not label agent self-review as human or fresh-viewer review. A Disney-quality target never becomes a proven result simply because this checklist was followed. Deliver the actual evidence and limitations, and keep publication separately authorized.
