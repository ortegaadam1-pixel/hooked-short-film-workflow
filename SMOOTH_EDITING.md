# Smooth editing — v2.3 production standard

Required companion to [SYSTEM_PROMPT.md](SYSTEM_PROMPT.md), [PERFORMANCE_AND_FINISH.md](PERFORMANCE_AND_FINISH.md), and [PRODUCTION_TEMPLATE.md](PRODUCTION_TEMPLATE.md). Applies to every genre and art style. The current request changes future production instructions only; leave the completed Christmas film unchanged.

**Goal: make the viewer follow the story and feel the performance without being distracted by the edit.** A hook needs a compelling event, not a quota of cuts. Keep urgency where the story needs it and room to breathe where meaning needs it. Deliberately abrupt horror, comedy, montage, or graphic animation is allowed when specified and observed to work; accidental choppiness is a defect.

## 1. Separate the observed problem from its possible causes

User feedback on The Gift That Cried v07: the result was acceptable but a little too choppy. The actual edit record shows multiple source-speed changes, silent intervals removed from picture and audio together, and a cut to a slightly cropped continuation of one source. Those are plausible contributors, not a proven explanation of each perceived rough moment. No audience study or new sound-on comparison has been performed for this documentation revision.

The previous workflow already asked for motivated cuts and smooth joins. The execution gap was allowing aggressive timing changes without a specific continuity decision. This revision makes the edit order, protected action, audio independence, and acceptance record explicit. It does not claim the written process alone produces smoothness.

## 2. Plan connected coverage before generating

- Write the physical or emotional action as preparation/intention → action → result → response. These phases may share one shot. Do not generate separate shots just to give each phrase a new image.
- Prefer one usable continuous performance for an action that can be read in one setup. Add an insert or reaction when it reveals needed information, conceals a genuine ellipsis, or changes viewpoint meaningfully.
- Plan each outgoing and incoming view together: screen direction, action phase, eyeline, prop state, focal position, light, and camera motion. Ask where the viewer is looking immediately before and after the cut.
- Request usable motion before and after the intended cut. As a starting coverage allowance, about 0.5–1 second at either end can help when the generation mode permits it; this is a production heuristic, not a compulsory retained hold or a supported-duration claim.
- Make overlapping coverage describe the same event and state. Use matching references and adjacent-frame conditioning only when the actual tool supports them. Do not assume text prompts guarantee matching animation.
- Budget for thought, recognition, and the final emotional reward in the animatic. Simplify the number of events before trying to compress all of them into a shorter edit.

## 3. Clean speech first, then establish picture timing

For an existing approved narration, preserve its identity and every required word. Work on a copy. Keep original audio and picture recoverable.

1. Listen to full phrases and locate unnatural TTS gaps, breaths, dramatic silences, and meaningful inflection. Silence detection identifies candidates only; it cannot decide what is disposable.
2. Trim only a demonstrated speech defect. Preserve sentence rhythm, consonant releases, inhalations where natural, and purposeful suspense. Inspect and listen to each repaired phrase in context.
3. Use small audio fades/crossfades or continuous room tone where needed to prevent clicks or noise-floor jumps. Choose their length by the material; do not overlap words or apply one fixed fade to every cut.
4. Save and measure the cleaned narration version before final picture timing. If listening is unavailable, retain uncertain intervals and mark naturalness review PENDING.
5. **An audio pause deletion is not permission to delete the corresponding picture frames.** Once an existing picture edit exists, changing narration requires an explicit picture-timing review, not a global linked ripple delete.
6. For dialogue, preserve true synchronization and character reactions. Separate audio edits from picture cuts only where that is appropriate, using genuine coverage or a deliberate sound bridge.

An approved narrator does not need to be auditioned again just because the workflow has a two-delivery development exercise. Reuse approved performance evidence and fix only the demonstrated issue.

## 4. Assemble complete actions at their intended playback speed

Build the first picture assembly from actual usable actions with the selected narration. Keep source performance at its intended native playback speed by default. Respect deliberately stepped or selective animation; do not force smooth interpolation onto a chosen style.

Mark essential event anchors such as contact, jump, landing, discovery, and recognition. Align those events to meaning and dramatic intention; do not move every shot boundary to the first word of a sentence. Narration may continue over a cut, and a shot may carry several phrases. Literal action narration must agree with the visible action; rewording approved text requires the applicable user authorization.

Protect preparation, physical contact, follow-through, and reaction when they carry meaning. If shortening removes one, restore it or redesign coverage. Avoid a hard cut between nearly identical framings solely to reset timing. A small digital crop of the same continuous shot is not automatically new coverage; keep it continuous or make a genuinely motivated reframing.

**Do not compute a new speed for each shot merely to fill assigned narration slots.** Do not use freeze frames, repeated frames, artificial slow motion, or blanket speed-up to cover missing material. When a mismatch remains, try in order:
- Shift an editable boundary while preserving the event anchor and clear cause/effect.
- Let the spoken phrase bridge a continuous action or an established reaction/detail shot.
- Remove a redundant shot or simplify an unapproved script/beat within scope.
- Obtain suitable coverage or reperform only the affected material within production authorization.
- Use deliberate retiming only if the effect itself is justified. Record source range, actual speed, reason, and normal-speed observation of contact, weight, and interpolation artifacts. Do not silently retime synchronized speech.

A purposeful held drawing can be valid animation. A duplicated frame used merely to fill a hole is not the same creative decision.

## 5. Make sound and picture flow across boundaries

Default to a clean cut when continuity works. Keep ambience or a suitable sound bed continuous across same-location shots so every image change does not also reset the sound. Preserve meaningful silence; continuous ambience does not mean compulsory music.

Where appropriate and supported:
- **J-cut:** the next scene's sound begins before its picture, preparing the change.
- **L-cut:** the outgoing scene's sound continues after the picture changes.
- Narration can span shots without restarting at every boundary; this alone is not necessarily a scene-based J/L cut.
- A motivated sound effect can connect an action to its consequence without hiding confusing geography.

Use a dissolve for a deliberate change in time, place, or emotional connection when its overlap reads clearly. Do not apply a universal dissolve duration or dissolve every join. Double faces, duplicated limbs, ghosted contacts, and muddied reveals are reasons to use a clean cut or change coverage. Adding transition effects is not the first remedy for choppiness.

Do not claim sound bridges, overlapping tracks, or crossfades were implemented when the editor does not support them. Record the limitation and use the simplest feasible continuous sequence.

## 6. Review rhythm across a whole dramatic unit

A two-shot method test remains useful for a difficult join. It is insufficient to judge the film's overall rhythm. Before the final render, review a continuous representative sequence containing setup, action, result, and reaction with the real narration. A section around 8–12 seconds is often useful; use the complete shorter scene when appropriate. This duration is an editorial starting point, not a validated threshold or a new footage-generation quota.

Review the whole assembly before adding extra pace. Vary shot duration by dramatic purpose. Alternate urgency, comprehension, and emotional release as the story requires; do not alternate them mechanically.

If a passage feels rushed, compare the existing cut with a breathing-room version that restores the missing action or reaction and/or removes a redundant cut. If it drags, compare with a tighter version. Change only the passage with a demonstrated issue; do not always choose the shorter version or rebuild an entire film to perform the test.

For each join, use enough lead-in and lead-out to see the complete moving action, not just boundary frames. Record the real artifact, timecode, observed issue, revision, and result. A new shot is not necessary if existing performance already advances emotion or anticipation.

## 7. Captions follow the performance

Create and align captions after the chosen picture/sound timing is stable. Use semantic phrases, sensible reading time, restrained styling, and no compulsory word-by-word bounce. Do not create a cut simply because a caption changes.

Check every caption event, including the intervals between contact-sheet samples. Keep placement stable within a shot or dramatic unit where practical; move only to protect faces, hands, contact, props, or the reveal. Verify the entire moving path, not just one frame. Avoid a caption jumping position in the middle of a phrase. Captions and subtitles must preserve exact spoken meaning and must not reveal a surprise early.

If picture or speech timing changes, regenerate or remap the subtitle timing against the final version and check for overlaps, gaps, and clipped final words. Subtitle timestamps are not proof that the audio is complete.

## 8. Smoothness acceptance and records

Record these separately:
- Complete action and cause/effect are readable at the intended speed.
- Action phase, gaze, screen direction, prop state, and focal placement connect across each relevant join.
- Retiming and frame holds are absent or specifically justified with observed motion findings.
- Speech phrasing and breaths feel natural; no unintended clicks or abrupt ambience resets.
- Holds preserve anticipation or emotion; no compulsory cut interval or minimum cut count was used.
- Captions remain readable without covering the action during motion.
- The conclusion includes the complete last spoken word, visible outcome, and a purposeful emotional release.

Watch the exact export continuously at normal speed with sound, and perform a separate phone-size/muted check for its own purpose. Record who actually watched/listened and the exact export identifier or hash. Metadata, ASR, source-range arithmetic, contact sheets, and successful encoding support technical checks; none approves smooth rhythm or emotional satisfaction. When continuous review is unavailable, deliver REVIEW with that item PENDING, and do not claim the film is creatively approved.

For local-file finishing when authorized, verify dimensions, square pixels, frame rate, compatible H.264/yuv420p and AAC delivery where requested, full decode, ending, and measured levels. These technical checks supplement the creative review and must never force a change to an intentionally chosen medium.

## 9. Reusable edit record

| Beat / join | Protected action and reaction | Source ranges / final timing | Native speed or justified change | Picture transition and reason | Independent audio edit / bridge | Caption clearance through motion | Normal-speed sound-on observation / reviewer / status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Record the cleaned narration version, event anchors, candidate silence repairs and reasons, rhythm comparison if needed, whole-sequence playback method, exact export, and remaining limitations. If the user reports choppiness, reopen this record and investigate specific joins before prescribing more cuts or more effects.
