# Believable film craft and controlled revisions — v2.4

Required companion to [SYSTEM_PROMPT.md](SYSTEM_PROMPT.md), [SMOOTH_EDITING.md](SMOOTH_EDITING.md), and [PERFORMANCE_AND_FINISH.md](PERFORMANCE_AND_FINISH.md). These lessons apply across genres, media and lengths within the current brief. They are production judgments learned from iterative film work, not a proven retention formula.

## 1. Earn attention with a readable event

Give the opening a situation, an action or discovery, and a reason to want what follows. Connect its promise to the film's eventual payoff. Spectacle can establish a world or character, but does not by itself establish emotional investment. An intimate reaction can be a stronger hook than a large action scene. Do not force every film to open with a flyby, chase, shout, fast cut, or surprise.

When improving an approved opening, develop the requested action in its actual available time. Protect title legibility, face readability, the transition into the body, and the existing ending. A camera move or enlargement of a still does not demonstrate a living performance. Inspect source lead-ins for accidental frozen holds before selecting the usable action. Deliberately held drawings and restrained acting remain valid when appropriate to the chosen medium.

## 2. Make motion physically and emotionally coherent

Before generating a difficult shot, identify who initiates movement, what follows, which objects are connected, where contact occurs, and the expected result. Specify one dominant action and a motivated camera choice. Review the whole moving trajectory, not only the start and end poses.

- Locomotion needs articulated movement appropriate to the creature or vehicle, momentum, contact and weight where applicable. Floating a rigid group through space does not establish running, flight or a landing.
- Connected objects must keep their functional order, attachment and direction. A towing vehicle must not pass through its load; a hand must not lose the object it is carrying.
- Approach, contact, load transfer, follow-through and settling must form a plausible action. Avoid group motion that treats independent bodies as one rigid tilted plane.
- A character's eyeline, hesitation and reaction should have an intelligible target. Do not retain inert staring at an incidental prop merely to fill a narration slot.
- An apparent disappearance requires inspection through the occlusion or screen edge. Being outside a close framing is not proof that an object vanished.

Simplify difficult choreography before adding more prompt instructions. Use an established wide view for geography/count, a close view for expression/contact, then a result or reaction when this preserves causality. Motivate what is offscreen; do not use tighter coverage to hide a story-critical action that has never been established. Do not reattempt the same failed complex shot unchanged.

## 3. Treat continuity as persistent state

Maintain references for faces, proportions, costume, voices, significant props, set geometry and lighting. Track object count, location, ownership and condition across every relevant shot. A room's walls, doors, windows and furniture are part of its identity, not disposable backgrounds. Mechanical actions must retain the same construction: opening a sash must not turn it into a hinged window halfway through the action.

Map entry and exit states across shots. An action begun at a window should continue there until the character visibly leaves. A takeoff should continue from the established departure location unless an understandable transition establishes the change. Viewpoint shots must show what the observing character could plausibly see.

Compare additions against the actual medium: a glossy rendered object can break a painted world even when its color matches. Check texture, edges, perspective, occlusion, light and motion together. Separate layers must feel as though they occupy the same scene.

For composites, inspect foreground masks, stationary architecture and subject boundaries throughout motion. Watch for walls sliding independently, snapping crops, unstable shutters, edge halos and drifting overlays. Stabilization, warping, depth motion, background replacement and reframing can introduce defects into previously accepted footage. Compare against the last approved export before prescribing regeneration. If a new processing step caused the defect, repair or remove that step first.

## 4. Give effects a source, a path and a consequence

Fantasy can break ordinary physics while obeying the film's established rules. Show the initiating action, where the effect originates, its destination, and what remains afterward. A transported object should arrive and persist; a filled container should not become empty in the next view. A transformation should preserve spatial relationships unless the story deliberately changes them.

Contain fire, water, smoke and other effects within intended scene boundaries. A stray flame on a rug changes the story into a house fire, even if the prompt called it harmless magic. Preserve the effect's intended meaning and consequences. Review its integration at actual speed and across adjoining shots, including quieter intervals after the spectacular moment.

## 5. Make the visible world audible

Create a cue sheet from actual onscreen actions. Important contacts, footsteps, mechanisms, weather, breathing, laughter and calls need appropriate sound unless silence is a deliberate choice. Verify the effect is heard in the final mix, not merely present on a track.

Match perspective: distant outdoor activity should not sound like a dry close recording inside a room. Place the cue on the visible event or intended reaction trigger. A sound that wakes a character must plausibly precede the reaction. Literal narration cues and visible actions should agree unless the offset is intentional and understandable.

Preserve speech intelligibility. Listen to full phrases while clothing rustle, impacts or laughter play; reduce competing effects dynamically rather than drowning out words. Match vocal intention to performance, distance and setting. When an approved narrator remains, add or replace character dialogue only within authorization. For visible speech, verify the mouth against the final actual recording; an open mouth or moving beard is not proof of lip sync.

Music is optional. A no-music brief is a firm constraint, including generated clips that may contain unintended score. Ambience, effects, silence and vocal performance can carry emotion without a music bed. Protect sound tails, complete final words and the ending through black. A cover or call-to-action, when requested, should fit the film's world and be readable at intended viewing size without displacing the emotional ending.

## 6. Preserve approved work during a correction

Before editing, record the approved source version, requested change, affected ranges, adjacent joins and material that must remain unchanged. Keep source audio, references, projects and previous exports recoverable. A new instruction to remake rejected work overrides old preservation preferences for that scope; a request to change one hook does not authorize rebuilding the accepted body.

Change the smallest region that solves the observed problem, including necessary handles and sound tails. Preserve approved words, voice identities, footage and design outside it. Reuse accepted performances and references; do not repeat auditions or replace good shots without a demonstrated reason. When working on a correction, reuse existing development evidence instead of restarting premise comparisons or bulk generation.

For local finishing where available, use suitable stream copying or other preservation methods to avoid unnecessary reencoding. Verify claims at their actual level: file hashes establish file identity; packet payload/timestamp comparison establishes compressed-stream preservation; decoded-frame or PCM comparisons establish the corresponding decoded equality. One does not automatically prove another, particularly across codec priming, decoder history, timing and container changes. Technical identity does not establish creative quality.

Inspect the changed sequence and its neighboring joins. Check a complete fresh export for playback, captions, sound and the ending before quality approval. Preserve the prior review's exact artifact and scope; do not claim an old full-film review covers new footage. If full review is unavailable, record the changed-region evidence and the pending complete review separately. Delivering a review copy does not certify it as approved.

## 7. Resolve notes with evidence

Keep a timecoded issue ledger: observation, suspected cause, evidence, planned repair, actual result and remaining uncertainty. Investigate a user-reported defect even when an AI reviewer missed it. Conversely, verify an AI criticism before regenerating accepted material. Distinguish actual identity drift from expression, camera motion from moving walls, reflections from escaping effects, and a cut or occlusion from continuous morphing.

Use [GEMINI_REVIEW.md](GEMINI_REVIEW.md) when an additional audiovisual diagnostic is useful and authorized. Treat its findings as candidates to investigate. Record whether each was retained, corrected, retracted or remains uncertain. An AI review is not a human audience study, a certification of quality, or proof of uninterrupted watching.
