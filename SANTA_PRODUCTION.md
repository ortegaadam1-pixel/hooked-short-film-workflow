# Santa Claus image and video production — conditional module

**Use this module only when generating or correcting Santa Claus reference images or video clips, including his reindeer, sleigh and associated magical actions.** It is not a default style, character, sound or staging prescription for other films. A Christmas setting alone does not activate it. The current brief and approved designs control; do not impose a red suit, a particular narrator, eight reindeer, miniature scale or a poem-specific gesture on a different approved Santa story.

These are production lessons from observed failures and repairs, not a guarantee that a generation model will obey a prompt. Apply [FILM_CRAFT_AND_REVISIONS.md](FILM_CRAFT_AND_REVISIONS.md) and [SMOOTH_EDITING.md](SMOOTH_EDITING.md), and inspect the resulting images AND moving footage.

## 1. Lock the design and flight arrangement before animation

Reuse approved face, expression, costume, sleigh, sack and team references. Preserve Santa's age, proportions, beard/moustache, hat, clothing materials and voice. Keep the chosen medium consistent across Santa, deer, harness, toys, architecture and effects. Do not replace an accepted painted design with glossy 3D props or a different Santa simply because a new image is attractive.

For the traditional eight-reindeer team, use exactly eight deer in **four pairs, one pair behind another**, ahead of one sleigh. State a different approved count explicitly when the brief calls for it; do not silently add a ninth animal. In a miniature-team brief, keep the entire rig's scale coherent relative to rooftops, chimneys and Santa.

Define the functional order in world space:

> Front, toward the direction of travel: lead pair | second pair | third pair | rear pair | harness attachments | sleigh and Santa at the rear.

Describe actual screen direction separately. In a left-to-right side view, the leaders are furthest right and the sleigh is behind them on the left. In a right-to-left side view, the leaders are furthest left and the sleigh is behind them on the right. The team always pulls the sleigh; the camera angle never changes that functional order.

Inspect a useful wide or three-quarter reference at full size before animation:
- Count eight heads/bodies and resolve ambiguous overlaps. Antlers are not a reliable counting method.
- Confirm the four-pair arrangement, correct facing direction, plausible anatomy and clear separation of independent legs.
- Check that the sleigh is behind the team, not alongside the leaders or towing deer behind it.
- Show a readable functional harness: traces connect the team to the sleigh; Santa holds steering reins. Lines must not loop around antlers, cut through bodies or attach randomly to backs.
- Leave enough space for the intended approach, turn, descent or exit. A correct static pose must also be an achievable starting state for the action.
- Keep every approved reference's exact role clear: a library thumbnail is not proof that it is selected as the starting frame or consistency reference.

Reject or correct an incorrect reference before animating it. Do not expect a video prompt to repair extra animals, reversed order or impossible harness geometry.

## 2. Flying means articulated forward travel

Specify airborne forward movement, natural alternating leg cycles, modest independent body motion, flowing fur and a connected sleigh following the team's path. The chosen style may simplify motion, but the deer should not behave like rigid ornaments sliding through the sky when a flying gallop is required.

Review the complete trajectory:
- Each deer keeps its identity and position in the team; body parts must not merge, multiply or disappear mid-frame.
- Traces remain plausibly tensioned without stretching, snapping or letting the sleigh overtake the deer.
- Forward travel and background parallax agree. Camera tracking alone cannot establish that the team is galloping.
- Turns follow a coherent curve. Leaders change heading first and the following pairs and sleigh follow the path; do not rotate the entire team as one rigid tilted board.
- A close camera pass preserves perspective, occlusion and order. Animals leaving the frame are different from animals dissolving before reaching an edge.
- Keep the team airborne until the planned landing. Do not unexpectedly substitute a forest-ground run for an established rooftop departure.

Inspect the first usable source frames for an unintended frozen lead-in. Select a moving range with usable action handles; do not stretch or freeze the clip to fill a narration window.

Never reverse playback to manufacture a return flight, landing or takeoff. It reverses gait and cause/effect. If a whole shot is mirrored for a legitimate opposite screen direction, inspect the complete rig, lighting, geography, text and neighboring cuts; do not flip individual animals or change orientation mid-shot.

## 3. Stage a readable rooftop arrival

Establish the destination house, pitched roof, chimney, approach direction and usable landing area. Preserve that architecture through wide, runner and hoof views. Size the roof and rig plausibly; if the planned full team cannot fit, redesign scale/staging within the brief instead of morphing the roof into a flat ledge.

Use a shallow forward descending approach with momentum and a controlled reduction in speed. Lead hooves meet the snowy roof first, then the following pairs settle in sequence with individual hooves staggered naturally rather than landing as rigid units, and the sleigh runners contact and slide a plausible short distance behind them. Preserve weight transfer, footing and a settling reaction. This is staged fantasy flight, not a requirement to simulate an ordinary aircraft.

Reject an unexplained hover followed by a straight vertical group drop when a natural angled landing is requested. Reject hooves penetrating chimneys or shingles, feet sliding without grip, lead deer apparently falling off the roof, or an entire group sinking through snow. Chimney smoke originates at the chimney, never from a deer like exhaust.

Include an established wide view showing the required complete team, sleigh and Santa when count/geography matters. Then use matched closer contact views. A hoof insert alone does not establish that all eight arrived. Preserve readable preparation, contact and follow-through; do not cut from a stationary roof pose directly into a contradictory falling pose.

## 4. Take off from the roof that was established

Begin from the actual landed arrangement on the same roof. Santa's cue, the team's preparation, forward drive, changing hoof contacts and lift should connect. The lead pair lifts first along the forward climbing path, followed by the other pairs; runners release the roof after the sleigh moves forward. Let independent bodies follow the curve with believable spacing, rather than remaining coplanar on one rising slab.

All animals face and move away from Santa in the direction of travel. Do not let them turn toward him while the sleigh slides backward off the roof. Keep the sleigh behind its team throughout. A cut to a new location requires an understandable transition; it cannot silently move a roof launch to snowy ground.

## 5. Give a flyby room to turn and a face room to act

A wide shot can establish the eight-deer team crossing the sky. For a double-back, plan a continuous broad turn when feasible or let the team turn beyond the edge before returning in a new forward-moving pass. Do not fake it with reversed video. Keep the title and all important animals unobstructed through both passes.

For a camera-facing payoff, separate the hard jobs when needed: establish the complete rig in a wide shot, then use a motivated three-quarter close approach with the team already ahead and offscreen. Taut reins can continue beyond the frame toward the established team. Do not generate extra deer around Santa's face merely to fill a close composition.

Santa's face should become clearly recognizable before he passes. Protect expression, normal mouth shapes and identity under motion blur; avoid an exaggerated mouth or rapid head turn that changes his design. Keep the actual sleigh moving toward and past the lens rather than only zooming the image. If an exit is requested, show the whole sleigh clear the edge and retain a short useful sound tail. Do not cut away with half the sack still on screen and call the exit complete.

Use the approved voice where one exists. A required “Ho! Ho! Ho!” must be an actual warm Santa vocal performance, not a donkey-like effect or merely the narrator describing laughter. Check actual words, tone, timing and visible mouth motion. Do not assume three syllables in a prompt guarantee three in the output.

## 6. Keep Santa's indoor magic coherent

Apply only the actions required by this story. If the script requires a finger beside the nose, show that readable deliberate gesture before the magical ascent; do not substitute crawling up the chimney. If Santa enters or exits through a roaring fire, establish a clear magical passage contained within the same hearth. He must not crawl through ordinary flames, ignite a rug or appear beside an unrelated chair while the effect rises elsewhere.

Keep fire, sparks, shadows and architectural boundaries integrated throughout the shot. The room must not slide or reset when an effect is added. Retain the established chimney/hearth axis across arrival and exit.

Gift magic has a source, route and persistent destination: toys emerge from the actual sack, travel toward the intended stockings or tree, arrive and remain. Filled stockings stay visibly filled in the following view. Toys must read as recognizable toys in the approved medium rather than ambiguous blocks or unrelated overlays. If pipe smoke is required by the exact text, originate it at the pipe bowl and form the intended translucent wreath; it is not a default prop for every Santa film.

## 7. Sound and narration cue checks

Build cues from the actual edited actions: airborne wind and bells, roof contacts and snowy hoof taps, runner contact, Santa's whistle, laughter, magical transitions, and contained fireplace sound as applicable. Match distance and perspective. A roof clatter that causes a reaction must precede it; a whistle tied to a spoken mention must occur at the intended cue, not arbitrarily earlier. Check every whistle separately.

Protect narration from brushing, sack rustle, effects and laughter. A boisterous character call needs an appropriate delivery without replacing approved narration elsewhere. Use the final supplied character recording for any lip-sync work and verify the actual result. Preserve no-music instructions, including generated source sound. Finish the last word and sound tail without an abrupt cutoff.

When adapting exact narrated text, show moonlit snow from a plausible observer viewpoint during that line; reveal Santa at the intended mention. A withdrawing-head line requires the observer to still be at the window before turning inside. These are poem-specific examples of picture/word agreement, not mandatory scenes in other Santa stories.

## 8. Adaptable prompt examples

Replace bracketed fields, use the approved references and generate only the action needed. These templates express intentions; accept only inspected results.

**Wide flight reference**

> [Approved medium, ratio and identity references]. Wide three-quarter airborne view above [established location]. Exactly eight individually readable reindeer in four paired rows, all facing [screen direction], ahead of one [approved sleigh] carrying [approved Santa and sack]. Lead pair furthest forward, rear pair closest to the sleigh. Plausible harness traces connect the team to the sleigh, with steering reins in Santa's hands. Clear bodies, independent legs and consistent scale. [Relevant light and roof/chimney geography]. One physically coherent starting instant, no lettering.

**Connected flight or roof action**

> S[ID], [one continuous action: cruise / broad turn / descending landing / forward roof takeoff]. Start from the selected correct reference. Team travels [direction/path]; articulated flying gallop, independent leg cycles and natural spacing. Leaders guide the curve, following pairs and connected sleigh follow; the sleigh never overtakes the team. [For landing: shallow forward descent, successive hoof contacts, runners contact and settle.] [For departure: forward preparation, sequential lift, runners release from this same roof.] [One motivated camera choice]. Preserve count, identity, anatomy, harness and geography throughout. Sound: [actual bells/wind/roof contacts/cue], [no music when required]. Retain useful preparation and follow-through.

**Santa close pass after the established team**

> Continue the exact approved Santa/sleigh image in [medium]. The established team is ahead beyond this tight framing; reins lead toward [offscreen direction]. Sleigh advances toward the fixed camera in three-quarter view. Santa gives [readable restrained expression] and says [exact approved vocal], with natural mouth articulation and stable face/costume. Sleigh passes beside the lens and exits completely through [edge], leaving [clear established sky]. Actual forward travel, no digital zoom substitute. [Light wind/bells, vocal tail, no music if required]. Do not add deer in this close framing.

## 9. Acceptance and repair ledger

For the affected shot and joins, record reference IDs, intended/observed deer count, functional order, gait, harness, path, contacts, persistent architecture/props, Santa identity/face, audio cue timing and exact export time ranges. Review both the reference image and the actual moving result; a correct reference does not approve video.

When something fails, compare the source and composite against the approved version. Repair the demonstrated cause or simplify coverage before generating the whole sequence again. Preserve accepted flight and landing shots during an unrelated interior or voice correction.

Use [GEMINI_REVIEW.md](GEMINI_REVIEW.md) for an authorized second diagnostic when necessary. Ask it to distinguish offscreen passage from disappearance, normal facial articulation from identity morphing, and camera motion from sliding architecture. Verify its findings rather than accepting a confident count or timestamp automatically. Keep sampled analysis distinct from uninterrupted normal-speed sound-on review; pending review stays pending. No checklist or prompt guarantees cinematic quality or audience response.
